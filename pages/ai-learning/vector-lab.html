Lesson 1 · Direction and length

A vector is a set of walking directions

"Walk 3 steps east, then 4 steps north." That instruction is the vector [3, 4]. It tells you which way to go and how far. A plain number like 5 only tells you how much.

Drag the blue arrow tip. On a keyboard, focus the grid and use the arrow keys.

    Lesson 2 · Combining and resizing

    Adding is doing one trip after another

    Adding = walk trip a, then from where you stopped, walk trip b. The green arrow goes straight from start to finish. Scaling is a volume knob: ×2 walks twice as far in the same direction, ×−1 turns you around.

      Lesson 3 · The one AI uses most

      The dot product asks: how much do two arrows agree?

      Like a shopping bill: multiply matching parts, then add them up. You get one number. Big and positive means the arrows point the same way. Zero means they're at right angles (unrelated). Negative means they point against each other. Divide by both lengths and you get cosine similarity, the score vector databases use to rank documents.

      Drag a and b. The orange band along a is b's "shadow" on a. The dot product = shadow length × length of a.
