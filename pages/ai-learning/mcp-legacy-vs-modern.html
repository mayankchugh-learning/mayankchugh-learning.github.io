The real timeline

Before the protocol details: when this matters, what already shipped, and why working servers are not being forced off the older model.

✅
Say this first

Servers built on the 2025-11-25 model still run, unchanged, today. This page is a map of the 2026-07-28 revision — not a correction of the lifecycle, handshake, or JSON-RPC you already know.

Nov 2025
Legacy spec shipsThe 2025-11-25 revision — initialize, capability negotiation, tools/resources/prompts, Streamable HTTP, and session-oriented HTTP.
28 Jul 2026
Modern spec is publishedThe 2026-07-28 revision is final. SDKs can speak it. Existing implementations were not switched off on that date.
Today
Adoption is opt-inLegacy keeps working. You move when a new capability is worth the engineering, not because a countdown forced you.
Jul 2027
Earliest a deprecated piece could leaveMCP's published rule: a named deprecation gets at least twelve months before it is even eligible for removal.
🔌
Why ecosystems still move slowly

A published spec is not a migration. Rewriting a live server means new code paths, regression tests, and production traffic you cannot treat as a lab. Teams adopt when the payoff is real — extra replicas, cheaper routing, clearer traces — not when a date appears on a changelog.

"You did not learn a discarded model. You learned the model that is still fully supported, with no deadline pushing you off it."

Dates and the twelve-month deprecation floor come from the official Model Context Protocol revisions dated 2025-11-25 and 2026-07-28.

The one big idea

Almost every later chapter is a consequence of this single change. Learn it once and the rest stops looking like a random feature list.

Legacy — 2025-11-25

Remember me

You introduce yourselves once. After that, the server keeps a conversation in memory and keys it with a session.

Modern — 2026-07-28

Describe yourself every time

There is no receptionist keeping a guest list. Each request carries who is speaking, which version, and what this call needs.

🏥
Hospital-ward analogy

On one ward, a single nurse keeps your chart at the desk. Convenient — until that nurse is on break, and the next person has to reconstruct your history from memory. On the next ward, your wristband holds the identifiers every station needs. Slightly more to scan at each door, but any nurse on any shift can pick up your care without waiting for the person who first admitted you.

That is why the modern model exists. A process that must remember a caller can only be scaled by cloning that memory. A process that reads a complete request can be scaled by adding another identical process behind a load balancer.

"Memory is convenient until the only person who holds it is busy."

What changed

Who remembers you

This is the hinge. Toggle the two models and feel how the same conversation is stored in two different places.

Sticky conversation

One desk owns your file

You open a ticket with one agent. They remember the earlier messages. If that connection drops, the next agent has an empty desk — nothing you said was written where a stranger can read it.

🏷️
On the wire

That is the older Mcp-Session-Id habit. The instance that accepted the first call is the instance that still knows you. Adding more servers behind the scenes is awkward, because context lives in one process.

Self-contained request

The file travels with you

You hand over a complete ticket: who you are, which job this is, and what you need next. Any agent who opens it can continue. The conversation is not stored in one person's head.

📦
On the wire

The modern HTTP model does not rely on a sticky session header. Identity, protocol version, and the work itself travel on the request, so any healthy replica can serve it.

💡
The trade

Yes, each request is a little heavier. In production that cost is usually cheaper than pinning every caller to one machine when traffic spikes.

What changed

Asking mid-task

If the server no longer keeps a private memory of you, how can it still ask a question while work is in flight?

🏦
Bank-counter analogy

A teller can ask for your ID while processing the withdrawal you already started. A teller cannot walk into the queue and start asking strangers for ID before anyone has approached the window. The question is allowed only because your request is already open.

RuleIn plain words
Work must already be openA server may elicit only while it is handling something the client asked for. No surprise questions out of nowhere.
Ask a real questionThe elicitation has to contain an actual question — not a vague "something is needed" placeholder.

"If the server is asking, it is because you already asked it to do something first."

What changed

Confirming and continuing

The server asked. You answered. With no sticky session, how does the work pick up again? Play the sequence.

📚
Library-hold analogy

You place a hold and leave. Later you come back with the hold slip. Any desk can fetch the book — it does not have to be the librarian who first wrote the slip. The paper, not the person, is the memory.

Resume after a mid-task question
1 · CallClient sends a tool call with everything the job needs.
2 · PauseServer asks one concrete question while that call is still open.
3 · ReturnClient comes back with the answer plus the original job details.
4 · FinishAny free replica can complete the work. Nobody sat waiting on a held socket.
Press play to walk the four hops.
⚡
What this unlocks

The instance that asked the question does not have to idle until you reply. When the answer arrives, whichever replica is free can finish — because the ticket still carries the work.

"Nothing was forgotten. It was written down well enough that a stranger could continue."

The small stuff

Faster routing

Two small labels on the request let traffic machinery decide where to send it without opening the body.

🏭
Warehouse analogy

A sorter does not unbox every parcel to read the invoice. It glances at the barcode on the outside and pushes the box down the right chute. That is what Mcp-Method and Mcp-Name are for a load balancer.

Header

Mcp-Method

Which JSON-RPC method this is — initialize, tools/call, resources/read, and so on.

Header

Mcp-Name

Which tool, resource, or prompt is being named, so you can pin or shard by capability.

The small stuff

Smarter caching

A server can now say how long an answer stays fresh, and whether that answer is safe to share.

🛒
Shelf-label analogy

A grocery tag that says "offer ends Sunday" saves you asking the clerk every visit. When Sunday passes, you look again. That is ttlMs. Whether the same price is for everyone in the aisle, or only for your loyalty card, is cacheScope.

FieldMeaning
ttlMsHow long the client may treat the result as fresh, in milliseconds.
cacheScopeShared across many callers, or private to this client.
MCP: What's Changing › The small stuff
The small stuff

Finding what broke

In a chain of hosts, clients, and servers, "something failed" is not an incident report. You need the hop.

✈️
Connecting-flight analogy

A passenger changes airlines twice. If the bag vanishes, "it's lost" does not help. One PNR that follows the bag through every carrier tells you which transfer dropped it. MCP now carries W3C trace context the same way.

When a tool call fans out through a gateway, a second MCP server, and an internal API, the same trace id lets you open one search and see the full path — not three disconnected log files.

Newer pieces

Apps on the protocol

This is already a named, shipped extension — not a slide about the future. It explains a lot of the newer vocabulary.

🔌
Grid-and-appliance analogy

The electrical grid is the platform. You do not live in the grid; you live in the appliances plugged into it. MCP is settling into that role: a socket standard, with most people spending their time in a hotel-booking app, a warehouse connector, or a finance tool built on top.

Naming

Reversed domains

Extensions are named like com.yourcompany.tool so two publishers cannot collide by accident.

Interaction

Real UI, not only text

An app can present a pick list, a booking form, or a confirmation — not just another paragraph of model output.

MCP: What's Changing › Newer pieces
Newer pieces

Gone vs going away

Two words that sound alike and are not. Be precise in interviews and in migration notes.

Removed

Not in the modern spec

The old handshake shape, ping, logging/setLevel, and the old session header are simply absent from 2026-07-28.

Deprecated

Still works, dated

Roots, Sampling, Logging, and the older HTTP+SSE transport keep working until at least July 2027 under the twelve-month policy.

⏰
The twelve-month floor

"Deprecated" is not "breaks next Tuesday." It is MCP's own rule that a named deprecation gets a minimum of twelve months from announcement before it is eligible to be removed. That clock started in July 2026. There is runway. There is not a fire drill.

MCP: What's Changing › Closing
Closing

What you should do

The honest answer, without hedging.

Today

Keep building on what runs

The 2025-11-25 model is what most production servers still speak. Lifecycle, JSON-RPC, and the three primitives you already practiced still apply directly. Moving is a choice, not a mandate.

Worth knowing

The shape of 2026-07-28

Self-contained requests. Mid-task questions answered by coming back with the ticket, not by holding a socket. A published deprecation policy for a short list of older pieces — nothing sudden.

"You do not need to rebuild today. You needed a clear picture of what is already published — and now you have one."
