What to notice

  • Chunk size trades precision for context: small chunks match sharply but lose surrounding meaning; big chunks keep context but blur the vector.
  • Overlap (dashed orange text) is repeated from the previous chunk so a sentence cut at a boundary still appears whole somewhere.
  • Recursive tries ¶¶ (paragraph), then ¶ (line), then spaces, so it respects structure before words. Fixed · Every char cuts mid-word.
  • Parent-Child (small-to-big retrieval): search the small child chunks, but hand the larger parent to the LLM.

Vector Embedding

Each chunk becomes a vector: a list of numbers that captures its meaning. Similar meanings get nearby vectors (famously, king − man + woman ≈ queen).

Chunks & vectors

What to notice

  • The model runs locally in your browser (Transformers.js + ONNX). The first load downloads a few tens of MB and is cached afterwards. Nothing you type leaves the page.
  • Vectors are normalised, so cosine similarity is just a dot product.
  • Switch the model: vectors and the map change. Vectors from different models live in different spaces and can never be mixed in one index.

Semantic Search

Ask a question. It is embedded with the same model as the chunks, then compared to every chunk by cosine similarity.

Top matches

What to notice

  • Try a question whose words are not in the text: semantic search still finds the right chunk, where keyword search would fail.
  • Toggle the query instruction prefix and watch the scores move. Arctic and BGE are trained with it; MiniLM is not.
  • Top-K is a precision/recall dial: more chunks give the LLM more evidence, but also more noise and more tokens.
