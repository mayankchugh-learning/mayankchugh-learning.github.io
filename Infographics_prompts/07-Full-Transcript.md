### Slide 7 — Solution Architect, Defined

So here's the definition, stripped down. A Solution Architect solves a problem within a system, or within a defined project boundary. You're given the "what" — build this system, fix this integration — and your job is the "how." It's deep, technical, and bounded. You will know, on any given day, exactly which system you're working on.

### Slide 8 — Enterprise Architect, Defined

An Enterprise Architect decides which systems should exist at all — and how that decision connects to business strategy. You're not handed the "what." You're often the one who has to figure out the "what," by asking: what is the business actually trying to become in three to five years, and what portfolio of systems gets us there without duplicating capability or creating a mess we can't unwind. It's broader, less code-facing, and far more about trade-offs than technical elegance.

### Slide 9 — Side-by-Side Comparison

Put side by side: Solution Architecture is project-scoped, system-deep, and answers "how do we build this." Enterprise Architecture is portfolio-scoped, strategy-facing, and answers "should this exist, and how does it fit." Neither one is more important than the other — they're different altitudes of the same profession.
